### 1.3.2. Mass

**Mass** is a measure of the amount of matter in an object and is a scalar quantity. It is often confused with weight, but while mass is constant regardless of location, weight is the force exerted by gravity on that mass. The unit of mass is the **kilogram (kg)**. 

Mass is crucial in determining how much an object will accelerate when a force is applied. For example, a car with a mass of $1000 \, \text{kg}$ will accelerate less than a car with a mass of $500 \, \text{kg}$ when the same force is applied. This relationship is vital in understanding motion and is articulated in Newton's Second Law.

### 1.3.3. Acceleration

**Acceleration** is defined as the rate of change of velocity of an object with respect to time. It is also a vector quantity, indicating both the magnitude and direction of the change in velocity. The unit of acceleration in the SI system is meters per second squared ($\text{m/s}^2$). 

For instance, if a car increases its speed from $20 \, \text{m/s}$ to $30 \, \text{m/s}$ in $5$ seconds, its acceleration can be calculated as follows:

$$
a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{30 \, \text{m/s} - 20 \, \text{m/s}}{5 \, \text{s}} = 2 \, \text{m/s}^2
$$

- **Example 1**: A car with a mass of 1,000 kg accelerates at \(2 \, \text{m/s}^2\). The net force acting on the car can be calculated as:
  $$
  F_{\text{net}} = m \cdot a = 1000 \, \text{kg} \cdot 2 \, \text{m/s}^2 = 2000 \, \text{N}

- **Example 2**: If a 5 kg object experiences a net force of 15 N, the acceleration can be calculated as:
  $$

## 4.1. Statement of the Third Law

Newton's **Third Law of Motion** states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. This fundamental principle highlights the intrinsic symmetry of forces in nature, where one body exerts a force on another, and simultaneously, the second body exerts a force of equal magnitude but in the opposite direction back on the first body. Mathematically, this can be expressed as:

$$
\vec{F}_{AB} = -\vec{F}_{BA}
$$

where \(\vec{F}_{AB}\) is the force exerted by body A on body B, and \(\vec{F}_{BA}\) is the force exerted by body B on body A.
